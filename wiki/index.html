    Featured Concept

    The Great Charter of 1847 established Toin as a federal constitutional republic. Drafted after decades of home rule, it unified the states under democratic principles and remains the nation's foundational document.

    Historical Note

    • The Dutch abandoned Toin in 1697 after two decades of tenuous presence.
    • The British rediscovered the archipelago in 1701 and began settlement at Fort Endeavour in 1703.
    • Toin gained home rule in 1822 and full independence with the Great Charter in 1847.

    May 3: In 1864, during the peak of the wet season, the Department of Public Works was formally established by the National Assembly—a rare example of legislative progress in Toin's storm season.

    Language Spotlight

    Táčakyáwt is the ancestral language of the Indigenous peoples of Toin. It relies on oral transmission and communal debate, with sacred memory encoded in carved wood rather than text.
